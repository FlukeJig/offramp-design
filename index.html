The quiet economics of attention

Marginal Review· 6 min
From your #1 source · 6:12 this morning

Attention is the only budget you spend every waking hour, and it never sends a statement. Most people meet their real priorities at midnight, in the gap between what they planned and what the feed decided.

Economists call this a revealed preference: you vote with your minutes, and the tally is brutal. The phone is not stealing your time; it is arbitraging your inattention, one notification at a time.

The quiet correction is not willpower. It is accounting. When the minutes are counted, the counting changes them.

Boredom used to be a room you had to sit in. You stared out the window, your mind wandered, and eventually it came back with something — an idea, a decision, a memory worth keeping.

Interconnected · 5 min
From your #5 source · 6:38 this morning

Plans age like milk; prototypes age like wine. The fourth year of this running argument lands on the same conclusion, with better evidence: teams that shipped weekly learned more than teams that planned quarterly.

The twist is why. Shipping is not speed — it is contact with reality, and reality is the only teacher that does not grade on a curve.
